🤔 What is a Decision Tree?

A decision tree makes predictions by asking a series of yes/no questions about the data. Like playing "20 Questions," each answer narrows down the possibilities until you reach a conclusion.

💡 Core Idea: At each step, find the best question to ask that splits your data into the purest groups possible. Keep asking questions until each group is pure (or small enough).
🍎 Fruit Sorting Analogy

Imagine sorting fruit: "Is it round?" → Yes leads to apples/oranges. "Is it orange-colored?" → Yes = orange, No = apple. Each question creates a branch, and the leaves are your final answers!

📏 How Does It Decide What to Ask?

  • Gini Impurity: Measures how "mixed" a group is. Pure groups (all one class) have Gini = 0.
  • Best split: Choose the question that creates the purest subgroups.
  • Recursive splitting: Repeat the process on each subgroup.
  • Stop conditions: Stop when groups are pure, too small, or max depth reached.
🎯 The Splitting Process

Think of each split as drawing a line (horizontal or vertical) that best separates different classes. The algorithm tries every possible line and picks the one that creates the cleanest separation!

🌲 Tree Structure

  • Root node: The first question (top of tree)
  • Internal nodes: Middle questions (branches)
  • Leaf nodes: Final predictions (ends of branches)
  • Depth: Number of questions from root to leaf
🎪 Max Depth Trade-off: Shallow trees (depth 1-2) are simple but may be too general. Deep trees (depth 4+) fit training data perfectly but may overfit. Depth 2-3 often works best!

📊 Advantages & Disadvantages

Advantages:

  • Easy to understand and visualize
  • No data scaling needed
  • Handles both numerical and categorical data
  • Shows which features matter most

Disadvantages:

  • Can overfit if too deep
  • Small data changes can create different trees
  • Biased toward features with more levels

🌍 Real-World Examples

  • Medical diagnosis: "Does patient have symptom X?" → guides treatment
  • Loan approval: "Is income > $50k?" → "Credit score > 700?" → Approve/Deny
  • Customer segmentation: Classify customers by behavior patterns
  • Email filtering: "Contains word 'free'?" → "Has attachments?" → Spam/Not spam
🤖 Why Trees Are Popular

Decision trees form the foundation of powerful ensemble methods like Random Forests and Gradient Boosting, which combine many trees to make even better predictions!
